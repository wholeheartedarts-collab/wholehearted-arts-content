## Executive summary

Agata's Studio Collection piece "The Time of Singing Has Come" is a
mixed-media painting on plywood — red poppies, fragments of music,
patterned papers, texture, and handwritten words — built around Song of
Solomon 2:12 and described by the catalog as capturing "the feeling of
creation waking again." The catalog also notes it draws on Agata's own
Polish heritage. The verse is a spring image: winter has passed, flowers
appear, and song returns to the land — a natural, non-political angle for
a faith-and-art post about joy that arrives after a season of waiting.

## Context: reading of Song of Solomon 2:12 in Christian devotional use

Song of Solomon 2:10-13 is widely read devotionally (independent of its
primary meaning as a love poem between bride and groom) as an image of a
season change — winter passing, new growth, and song returning — often
applied to seasons of personal waiting, grief, or dormancy giving way to
renewal. This is a common, mainstream devotional reading (not a fringe or
disputed interpretation) and fits the client's confirmed content pillars
(faith, healing, overcoming struggles by staying faithful to God)
without requiring any invented personal narrative.
