# Research: "A Field Full of Yes" (Studio Collection painting)

## Executive summary

"A Field Full of Yes" is a small (16" x 12"), in-stock Studio Collection
original by Agata May'kowska — a joyful, meadow-inspired floral piece in
layered greens, soft sky tones, pinks, reds, yellows, and blues. The
painting's own title ("Yes") lines up naturally with a real, well-known
scriptural theme — God's promises being answered with "Yes" — without
needing any invented backstory. Because the catalog description is a
product listing (not a personal story from Agata), the angle here stays
image-led and faith-reflective rather than narrating an anecdote she
hasn't confirmed.

## Risks, counterarguments, or limitations

- Thin narrative: the catalog description is purely descriptive (size,
  colors, mood) with no scripture or personal story written into the
  piece itself, unlike several other catalog entries (e.g. "Flower of
  Righteousness" has Philippians 1:9-11 written into the canvas). The
  "yes" framing here is the writer's own connection between the title
  and a real verse — clearly presentable as a reflection prompted by the
  title, not as a claim that Agata wrote the verse into the painting.
  Both platform drafts must be careful to phrase this as "the title put
  me in mind of..." rather than asserting the verse is literally part of
  the artwork.
- Only 1 in stock — any CTA mentioning availability must stay honest
  (it's limited, not a false-urgency sales push).
- No mockup image exists for this piece — the final image will be the
  flat product photo, which is accurate but less visually rich than a
  styled room shot. Flagged for human follow-up (Agata could add a room
  mockup later).

## Possible content angles

1. **"Yes" as a posture of faith** — tie the painting's title directly
   to 2 Corinthians 1:20: God's promises are answered with Yes, and the
   painting becomes a small, close-looking reminder of that. (Chosen
   angle — fits faith/overcoming-struggles pillar, art-collector
   audience.)
2. **Slow looking / small-scale art** — focus on the "tiny discoveries
   up close" quality of a small-format piece, inviting the viewer to
   slow down. (Secondary, visual, could work as an Instagram-only
   angle.)
3. **A joyful meadow in a season that doesn't always feel joyful** —
   using the piece as a seasonal (autumn) counterpoint/encouragement
   note. (Not chosen — could read as generic seasonal filler; the
   scripture-title connection is stronger and more specific.)
4. **For the shelf, not just the gallery wall** — the catalog's own
   framing ("perfect for a shelf, bedroom, entryway...") as a lower-key,
   practical collector's note. (Not chosen as primary — more
   product-flavored than reflective; could inform the CTA only.)

Angle 1 was chosen for both platforms, since it's the most substantive,
verse-anchored, and genuinely tied to this specific piece's own title
rather than a generic encouragement post that could apply to any
painting.
