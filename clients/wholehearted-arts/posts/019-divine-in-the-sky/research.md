# Research Brief: "Divine in the Sky" (Studio Collection)

## Executive summary

"Divine in the Sky" is a mixed-media sunset/sunrise painting from Agata
May'kowska's Studio Collection, made with fabric, acrylic, alcohol ink,
and gold leaf, with the text of Romans 1:20 worked into the horizon line
of the piece itself. The painting's own premise — that creation itself
testifies to God's existence — is a direct, verifiable echo of two
well-known scripture passages about creation declaring God's glory
(Romans 1:20 and Psalm 19:1). This gives the post a strong, low-risk
angle: reflection on "ordinary" sky moments (sunrise, sunset) as
everyday, accessible evidence of the divine, tied directly to the
painting's own stated intent rather than to any invented backstory.

  work's visual/material content and its scriptural anchor, but nothing

  to what the piece itself says and does, plus general, well-supported
  reflection on the "creation reveals God" theme — image-led rather than
  story-led, consistent with the skip_rules note about thin-narrative
  pieces.
- **Theological framing care**: Romans 1:20 is sometimes cited in

  "without excuse" clause, to stay consistent with brand-voice's warm,
  non-preachy tone.
- **No politics**: this topic has no political dimension; nothing to

## Possible content angles

1. **"Written into the horizon"** — the literal fact that Romans 1:20 is
   physically part of the painting's composition; the post can explore
   the idea of scripture literally embedded in a sunset.
2. **Ordinary sky, extraordinary evidence** — reflecting on how something
   as common as a sunrise/sunset is framed by Romans 1:20 as everyday,
   accessible testimony to God, tying to the "creation declares God's
   glory" theme (Psalm 19:1) without needing rare or dramatic experiences
   to notice it.
3. **Materials as metaphor** — fabric, alcohol ink, and gold leaf coming
   together into one image, mirroring how ordinary daily materials/
   moments (a commute, a kitchen window, a drive home) already hold this
   evidence, if a person is willing to look.
4. **A collector's-eye angle** — for the art-collector audience
   specifically: why a small, framed, signed piece for a home functions
   as a daily reminder/practice, not just decor (soft CTA toward
   viewing/DMing about the piece).
5. **A quieter, image-led version for Instagram** — given the thin
   personal-narrative material, an Instagram caption can lean almost
   entirely on the image + the two verses + a short reflective line,
   rather than trying to manufacture a longer story.

Recommended primary angle across platforms: **#2 (ordinary sky,
extraordinary evidence)**, since it's the most inclusive, avoids
overclaiming personal backstory, and cleanly supports all three
platforms without stretching thin material.
