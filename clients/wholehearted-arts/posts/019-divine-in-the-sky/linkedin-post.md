## Hook
Most days, the most convincing evidence of God I see doesn't come from a sermon. It comes from the sky outside my studio window.

## Body
I finished a piece recently called "Divine in the Sky" — a sunset painting in fabric, acrylic, alcohol ink, and gold leaf. Those materials don't usually get along. Fabric wants texture, ink wants to bleed, gold leaf wants to sit flat and catch light. Getting them to work together took a while.

But that struggle is part of the point. Romans 1:20 is actually written into the horizon line of the painting itself — it's not just a theme I attached afterward. The verse says God's power and nature have been "clearly perceived... in the things that have been made," ever since creation.

I think about that every time I catch a sunset from an ordinary place — a parking lot, a car window, mid-errand. Psalm 19 puts it simply: "the heavens declare the glory of God." Not a special occasion. Not a mountaintop. Just the sky, doing what it does, most evenings, whether anyone's watching or not.

That's the part I keep coming back to with this piece — you don't have to go looking for proof. It's already overhead.
