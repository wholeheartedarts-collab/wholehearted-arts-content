## Hook
There's a figure at the center of my newest piece, and it's not made of paint. It's made of torn fragments of Scripture — raised up, rough edges and all.

## Body
I didn't smooth those edges down on purpose.

"Heaven Declares" is built from layers of fabric, paper, and acrylic, and I left a lot of that paper and fabric intentionally rough. Not because I ran out of time or patience with it — because that's closer to the truth.

We're created beautifully. We also move through life marked by weakness, by process, by things still unfinished. Perfectly imperfect, if that's not too tidy a phrase for it.

Psalm 19 holds that tension too. Most people quote its opening lines — the heavens declaring God's glory without a word. I wanted to sit with the second half instead, the part about God's own Word:

"The law of the LORD is perfect, refreshing the soul... sweeter than honey, than honey from the honeycomb." (Psalm 19:7-10)

That's not a God who needs our fragments cleaned up before we show them to Him. Paul put it even more plainly: "we have this treasure in jars of clay, to show that the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us" (2 Corinthians 4:7).

So the figure in this piece is lifted, not hidden. What's fragmented gets offered. What's unfinished gets lifted. And somewhere in that offering, the heart gets made whole — not because it stopped being a jar of clay, but because of what's carried inside it.

That's the painting, in full: 40" x 30", fabric, paper, and acrylic on canvas, silver frame.
