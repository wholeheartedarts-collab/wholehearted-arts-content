## Hook
Some places look rugged until the light touches them.

That's the line I kept coming back to while finishing this piece — and it's where this post starts too.

## Body
I've been working on a new 20x20 painting called "Held in the Hights" (yes — typo on my own site; the piece itself says "Heights," and so will I).

The idea started simple: a surface that looks worn, uneven, almost empty — until the light hits it differently. Then you realize it was never empty. Just holy in a quieter way.

I kept thinking about Habakkuk 3:19 while I painted:

"GOD, the Lord, is my strength; he makes my feet like the deer's; he makes me tread on my high places."

What gets me about that verse is where it sits. Habakkuk doesn't open his book calm. He opens it asking God hard questions — why injustice keeps winning, why things aren't getting better (Habakkuk 1:2-4). The confidence in chapter 3 isn't naive. It's earned. He gets his footing on the high, difficult ground, not around it.

That's the painting, too. Not a smooth landscape. A rugged one — that turns out to be exactly where the grace was.
