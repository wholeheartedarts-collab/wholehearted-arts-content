- **Scripture anchor:** Psalm 27:1 (ESV) — "Of David. The LORD is my
  light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The LORD is the stronghold
  of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?" Verified directly against
  ESV.org and Bible Hub (see sources.md). The painting's inscribed text
  ("The Lord is my light and salvation") is a close paraphrase of the
  verse's opening clause, not a verbatim quotation — worth naming
  precisely rather than implying it's a full verbatim Bible verse
  painted onto the canvas.
- **Authorship and context of Psalm 27 (per Enduring Word commentary and
  general Bible-background consensus, independently verified):**
  Attributed to David. Widely read as written during a period of real
  danger and opposition in his life (many scholars point to the years
  he was pursued by Saul), expressing confidence in God's protection
  built on God's *past* faithfulness as the ground for trusting God's
  *future* faithfulness — not naive optimism, but confidence tested by
  actual adversity.
- **The psalm's shape (per Enduring Word):** moves from bold declaration
  of trust (vv.1-6) to an honest, unguarded prayer that admits fear and
  the feeling of being forsaken (vv.7-13), then closes with a call to
  "wait for the LORD" (v.14). The psalm doesn't pretend fear is absent —
  it pairs real fear with real trust, which is a more honest and
  relatable read than "trust erases fear."
- **Sunflower symbolism (general, not sourced from the artist — flag as
  visual observation, not an artist quote):** sunflowers are commonly
  associated with turning toward light (heliotropism) as a natural,
  visual echo of the "light" language in the verse. This is a reasonable
  observation to make about the *painting's own visual choice* (a
  sunflower specifically, not some other flower), but should not be
  dressed up as botanical fact asserted with authority — keep it as a
  simple, plain observation.
