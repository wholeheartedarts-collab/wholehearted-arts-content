## Hook

"Whom shall I fear?"

That's the question sitting at the center of a small piece I made recently — an 8-inch circle, a sunflower, and a line from Psalm 27 written straight into the paint.

## Body

It's a small piece with a big claim in it — and the more I sat with it, the more that felt like the point.

The words are a version of Psalm 27:1: "The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?" It's a psalm attributed to David, and scholars widely read it as coming from the middle of real danger in his life, not after it had passed — years, many believe, of being pursued by real enemies. And even inside that same psalm, a few verses later, David is honest that fear hadn't fully left him. He wasn't writing from the other side of being afraid. He was writing from inside it, and choosing trust anyway.

That's the version of faith I wanted this piece to hold. Not the version where fear disappears. The version where you name it, and answer it with something steadier.

I painted a sunflower for it — a flower that visibly turns toward light, which felt like the right image for a verse about not being afraid of the dark. Layered texture, a shimmer finish, the words circling the bloom like something you'd read every morning without needing to think about it anymore.

I've started calling it a small altar for the home. Something you could set on a shelf where you'll actually see it — not a statement piece, just a quiet one.
