## Hook
There's a moment when someone stands still in front of a painting a
little too long, and their face changes.

That's the moment I make art for.

## Body
I don't always know exactly what's happening for them in that moment.
Maybe something eases. Maybe something that's been sitting quiet for a
while finally gets permission to rise to the surface.

Researchers who study what it actually means to be "moved" by something
— tears, joy, or that strange mix of both at once — found that people
experiencing it aren't really in control of it. They're witnesses to it,
not the ones causing it. And that's part of what seems to make it feel
safe enough to let happen at all, instead of overwhelming.

That lines up with something I feel every time I'm building a piece.

One piece that carries that same idea is "Fear Not," a painting I made a
few years ago. It's a 36×36 inch mixed media painting — mirror, fabric,
acrylic, paper — with words from Isaiah 54 woven into it, layered in
small, quiet shapes across the background. The mirror fragments mean it
never looks quite the same twice; it shifts as the light in the room
shifts. I built it to feel less like decoration and more like an
interruption — the kind that's holy, not startling.

I don't get to see most people's faces when they stand in front of it.
But that's what I think about whenever I'm layering a new piece — the
hope that somewhere in all that texture and color, it meets someone
exactly where they are.

That's the whole reason I keep doing this.
