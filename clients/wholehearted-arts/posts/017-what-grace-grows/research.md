## Executive summary

"What Grace Grows" is a large (36x25") original floral piece in acrylic
and pastel from Agata's Studio Collection — layered blooms in pink,
lavender, blue, green, and gold that the catalog describes as "joyful,
tender, and alive," carrying "hope, abundance, and quiet praise." The
piece sits squarely in the client's confirmed art content pillar (faith,
emotion, healing, praising God) and pairs naturally with a well-known,
verifiable Gospel passage about flowers, growth, and provision —
Matthew 6:28-29 ("Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow...").
This is a single-painting, art-collector-audience topic; no AI-automation
angle applies.

## Scripture verification (external claim — verified against real,
citable sources since direct fetch to biblegateway.com/biblehub.com/
esv.org is blocked in this sandbox; verified instead via WebSearch result

- **Matthew 6:28-29** — verified via WebSearch snippets citing KJV, NIV,
  ESV, and RSV texts (sources: bible.com, esv.org listing, biblehub-style
  aggregator snippets, christianity.com). Representative text (ESV):
  "Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow: they neither toil
  nor spin, yet I tell you, even Solomon in all his glory was not
  arrayed like one of these." Part of the Sermon on the Mount, addressing
  anxiety over provision — Jesus points to flowers growing without
  striving as evidence of God's care. This is the strongest content
  angle for this painting: the literal image (flowers, growth, color)
  and the painting's own title ("What Grace **Grows**") map directly
  onto this passage's literal content and theme, with no forcing
  required.
- **2 Corinthians 12:9** — also verified via WebSearch (biblehub,
  biblestudytools, kingjamesbibleonline snippets). Representative text
  (ESV/NIV paraphrase blend from search): "My grace is sufficient for
  you, for my power is made perfected in weakness." Considered as an
  alternate angle (direct "grace" language matching the title) but set
  aside as the primary angle in favor of Matthew 6:28-29, since this
  verse's theme (strength through weakness) doesn't match the painting's
  actual visual content (joyful blooms, abundance) as directly — using
  it as the lead verse would require reading meaning into the piece the
  catalog description doesn't support. Noted here as a legitimate
  secondary/backup reference if a platform draft wants a second short
  line, but not required.

## Possible content angles

1. **Growth without striving** (primary, all platforms) — the painting's
   blooms as a visual echo of Matthew 6:28-29: flowers that grow, full
   and unhurried, without effort — as a picture of grace. Faith/emotion
   pillar, art-collector audience.
2. **Color as feeling** — sensory/process-style angle describing the
   layered pink/lavender/blue/green/gold palette and what "energy and
   gentleness together" looks like on canvas. Good for Instagram's
   visual-first format.
3. **Grace in the ordinary** — quieter, reflective angle: grace as
   something that grows quietly rather than arrives dramatically, tying
   the painting's "quiet praise" description to everyday faith. Good for
   Facebook's more conversational, community tone.
4. **A room, softened** — the painting as a way abundance/hope enters a
   physical space (the description's "brings a sense of hope, abundance,
   and quiet praise into a room" line) — lower priority since it edges
   toward interior-design framing rather than faith/art framing, and the
   piece has no styled room mockup to visually support this angle this
   run.

Angle 1 (with elements of 2 and 3 per platform) is used across all three
platforms, since this is a single-topic, art-collector piece with no
genuine platform-exclusive angle — mirroring across LinkedIn, Instagram,
