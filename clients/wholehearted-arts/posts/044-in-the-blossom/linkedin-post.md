## Hook

When I was little, I was obsessed with the story of Calineczka — Poland's version of Thumbelina. She was born inside the blossom of a flower.

I never outgrew that image. I just found a deeper reason for it.

## Body

As a kid, I used to imagine that we're all tucked inside a flower while we sleep — quietly becoming something better, something prettier, overnight. Pure childhood magic. No theology in it at all, just wonder.

Years later, that same picture came back to me, except now I saw it differently.

In Christianity, we talk about being reborn by the Spirit of God. But a seed doesn't become anything on its own. It has to land somewhere first.

Jesus told a whole parable about exactly that (Matthew 13) — seed scattered on a path, on rocky ground, among thorns, and on good soil. Only one of those four grows into anything. Not because the seed is different. Because the ground is.

That's the painting. "In the Blossom" — acrylic on canvas, 20"x16". It started as a little girl's daydream about a fairy tale, and it became a picture of what it actually looks like to be planted somewhere that lets you grow.

I think a lot of us know what it's like to be the seed on the path, or among the thorns — wanting to grow and just not having the conditions for it. This piece is my reminder, to myself as much as anyone, that good soil matters. And that it's never too late to find it.
