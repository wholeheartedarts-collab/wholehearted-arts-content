## Topic

The painting + its own stated theme: God's love and care reaching someone
through the steady, practical presence of friends during a season when
the artist "could not see the world clearly." Paired with Ecclesiastes
4:9-10's imagery of two people walking together so that one can lift the
other when they stumble.

## Executive summary

"Together we go further" is a 22"x30" Studio Collection piece whose own
catalog description is a direct, personal account: Agata made it during
a period of impaired vision, and names her friends' steady presence as
the way "God loved me through my friends." That maps naturally onto
Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 — "two are better than one... for if they fall, one
will lift up his fellow" — a passage specifically about the practical,
physical value of companionship in hardship, not abstract sentiment.

## Key points

- The painting's own language is the load-bearing material here: a
  season of "not being able to see the world clearly," friends who
  "walked with me so closely that my steps were secured," and the
  conclusion "God loved me through my friends." No embellishment needed.
- Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 (ESV) is the natural scriptural pairing: "Two are
  better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil. For
  if they fall, one will lift up his fellow." The verse's own image —
  one person's steadiness compensating for another's stumble — mirrors
  "my steps were secured" almost exactly.
- Proverbs 17:17 ("A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born
  for adversity") is a secondary, supporting reference for the same
  theme (friendship proving itself specifically in hard seasons, not
  easy ones) — usable as a brief echo, not the primary hook.
- This reads as a testimony about receiving help, not a lesson about
  giving it — the honest framing (per brand-voice.md's "genuine emotion,
  not performed emotion" rule) is gratitude for being carried, not a
  how-to about being a good friend.
