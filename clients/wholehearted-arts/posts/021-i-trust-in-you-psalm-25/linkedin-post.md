## Hook
I didn't paint Psalm 25. I let it become her.

## Body
In "I Trust In You My God," the words aren't written under the figure like a caption — they're written into her. Into the shape of her body, into the folds of her clothing. Psalm 25 isn't decoration on this piece. It's the material it's made of.

The psalm opens like this: "In you, LORD my God, I put my trust." Not trust offered after things resolve. Trust as the opening move, before the outcome is known.

David wrote this one as an acrostic — each line beginning with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet, moving through trust, honesty about his own struggles, and a request for guidance, letter by letter, like it had to be built patiently rather than said all at once. I think about that while I'm layering acrylic, alcohol ink, pen, and fabric onto a canvas. Trust isn't usually one brushstroke either.

Later in the same psalm, David asks for something other than rescue: "Show me your ways, LORD, teach me your paths." Not "get me out of this." "Teach me." That's the harder prayer, and the one I wanted this piece to hold.

## CTA
Which prayer do you find yourself praying more often — asking to be rescued, or asking to be taught? I'd genuinely love to hear in the comments.
