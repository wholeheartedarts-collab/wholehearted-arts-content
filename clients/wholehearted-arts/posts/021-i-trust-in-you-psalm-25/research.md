## Executive summary

Agata's mixed-media piece "I Trust In You My God - Psalm 25" is a visual
meditation on Psalm 25, with words from the psalm handwritten directly
into the form and clothing of a painted woman. Psalm 25 is one of
David's acrostic psalms (each verse beginning with a successive Hebrew
letter) built around trusting God, asking for guidance, and seeking
forgiveness in a season of trouble. The painting takes an entire psalm
about waiting on God through hardship and renders it as a human figure —
the prayer becomes the body. This is a strong, well-documented faith-pillar
piece with a real, verifiable scriptural anchor (not a thin-narrative case).

## Possible content angles

1. **The prayer becomes the body** — the concept of literally wearing
   scripture (words of trust handwritten into a painted figure's form
   and clothing) as a metaphor for letting a prayer shape you, not just
   say it. (Best fit: LinkedIn/Instagram, reflective/process angle.)
2. **Trust that doesn't wait for certainty** — Psalm 25:1-2's "in you...
   I put my trust" as an act of trust made before the outcome is known,
   not after. (Best fit: any platform — a relatable "putting trust first"
   framing.)
3. **Asking to be taught, not just rescued** — Psalm 25:4-5's "show me
   your ways... teach me your paths" as a different kind of prayer than
   asking for relief: asking for direction. (Best fit: Instagram/Facebook,
   more intimate/personal framing.)
4. **An acrostic prayer** — the structural detail that Psalm 25 moves
   through the Hebrew alphabet, letter by letter, as if trust has to be
   built patiently, one piece at a time — a possible bridge to the
   painting's own handwritten, built-up surface. (Optional angle, only
   if it doesn't feel forced in the final draft.)

Recommended primary angle: **#1 and #2 combined** — the painting makes
Psalm 25:1-2's declaration of trust visible as a worn, embodied thing,
not just a spoken line. Angle #3 (25:4-5) works well as a secondary/CTA
beat inviting reflection ("what would it look like to ask to be taught,
not just rescued").
