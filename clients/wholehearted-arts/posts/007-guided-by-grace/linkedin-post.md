## Hook

Psalm 34 wasn't written from a place of comfort.

David wrote it after pretending to be insane just to escape with his
life — spit running down his beard, faking madness in front of a king
who could have had him killed. Then he wrote a psalm of praise.

## Body

That's the scripture behind my newest large-scale piece, "Guided by
Grace."

I keep coming back to that detail because it changes how I read the
psalm's most famous line: "Taste and see that the LORD is good." That
wasn't written by someone with the luxury of hindsight and a settled
life. It was written from a cave, by a man who had just escaped a
Philistine city one step ahead of his own execution.

Praise, in that psalm, isn't a reward for things going well. It's what
came out of him in the middle of things going about as badly as they
could.

"Guided by Grace" is a large structural acrylic piece — Psalm 34's text
worked directly into the highlighted areas of the canvas, with an
illuminated figure at its center meant to hold both hope and strength at
once, not one after the other.

I think that's closer to how guidance actually works most of the time.
Not a clear road opening up ahead of you. More like light finding you
exactly where you already are — mid-struggle, not after it.
