## Executive summary

"Guided by Grace" is a large (48"x60") structural acrylic painting by
Agata May'kowska in the Atelier Collection, priced at $5,000. Per the
catalog, it integrates text from Psalm 34 into highlighted areas of the
canvas and depicts an illuminated figure symbolizing hope and strength
amid challenges, framed by the catalog description as "a reminder of the
uplifting power of faith and the beauty found in trusting the journey."
Psalm 34 itself is a real, well-documented psalm of David, written from a
place of desperation and deliverance — a strong, verifiable scriptural
anchor for a post about resilience and trust in God's guidance.

## Key points

- **Painting (per catalog, unverified live but a real product listing):**
  structural acrylic painting, 48"x60", $5,000, Atelier Collection.
  Psalm 34 text is worked into highlighted areas of the piece; an
  illuminated figure symbolizes hope and strength; free domestic

- **Psalm 34 — verified, real scripture, not client-supplied:**
  - Superscription: "Of David, when he changed his behavior before
    Abimelech, so that he drove him out, and he departed" — referring to
    the episode in 1 Samuel 21:10–15 where David, fleeing King Saul,
    feigned madness before Achish (called Abimelech, likely a royal
    title rather than a proper name, in the psalm's heading) to escape
    capture among the Philistines. [ESV.org; BibleRef.com;
    Enduring Word commentary]
  - The escape itself (1 Samuel 21:13, ESV): "So he changed his behavior
    before them and pretended to be insane in their hands and made marks
    on the doors of the gate and let his spittle run down his beard" —
    the specific, textually explicit detail behind "pretended to be
    insane." [ESV.org 1 Samuel 21:13; BibleHub]
  - Tradition places its composition in the aftermath of that escape,
    in the cave of Adullam, where David gathered with those who joined
    him — commonly read as a psalm of praise written out of genuine
    desperation, not comfortable circumstances. [Enduring Word
    commentary; Precept Austin]
  - Verse 8, one of the psalm's best-known lines: "Taste and see that
    the LORD is good; blessed is the man who takes refuge in him."
    Commentators note the Hebrew sense of "taste" and "see" go beyond
    literal sensation to mean personally experiencing and perceiving —
    an invitation to trust God through direct experience rather than
    secondhand assurance. [BibleStudyTools; GotQuestions; BibleRef]
  - The psalm's throughline is deliverance amid fear: David praises God
    "at all times," recounts crying out and being heard, and states that
    "the righteous cry out, and the LORD hears them; he delivers them
    from all their troubles" (v.17, common rendering) — consistent with
    the painting's stated theme of guidance and strength amid hardship.
- **Client fit:** matches the confirmed content pillars directly — faith,
  overcoming struggles by staying faithful to God, praising God. No
  political content, no sensitive-subject caution flags on this catalog
  entry.

## Possible content angles

1. **Deliverance under pressure** — Psalm 34 was written by someone
   escaping real danger, not from a place of ease; the painting's "hope
   and strength amid challenges" theme mirrors that origin directly.
2. **"Taste and see" as lived experience** — faith as something
   experienced firsthand (v.8), tying to the painting's illuminated
   figure and the idea of trusting the journey rather than just being
   told it will be fine.
3. **Guidance through the unfinished path** — framing "trusting the
   journey" (catalog's own phrase) as an ongoing practice, not a single
   moment of arrival — good for a reflective LinkedIn/Facebook angle.
4. **Image-led, short-form** — Instagram angle leaning on the visual
   (scale, illumination, integrated scripture) with a short caption
   inviting the viewer's own experience of the piece.
5. **Praise from a hard place** — connect the psalm's cave-of-Adullam
   origin to the general human experience of praising God from within
   hardship, not after it's resolved — a resonant angle for the "healing"
   and "overcoming struggles" pillars.

Angles 1, 3, and 5 are the strongest, most differentiated options and are
recommended for the writing skills.
