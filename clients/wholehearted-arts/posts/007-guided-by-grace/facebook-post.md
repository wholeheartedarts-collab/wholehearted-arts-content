## Hook

I want to tell you the story behind Psalm 34, because I don't think most
people know it — and it changes how you read the verse.

## Body

David wrote Psalm 34 after fleeing for his life. He'd escaped into
Philistine territory, and when he was recognized, he pretended to be
insane just to get out alive — spit running down his beard, scratching
at the gates like a madman. He got away. And then, from a cave, he wrote:

"Taste and see that the LORD is good."

That line gets quoted a lot, usually stitched onto something calm and
pretty. But it wasn't written from calm. It was written by someone who
had just barely survived, praising God in the same breath as the fear.

That's the scripture behind my newest large piece, "Guided by Grace" —
Psalm 34's words worked directly into the canvas, with a figure of light
at the center holding both hope and strength at once, not one after the
other.

I think that's usually closer to the truth of how grace actually shows
up. Not after the storm clears. Right in the middle of it, with you.
