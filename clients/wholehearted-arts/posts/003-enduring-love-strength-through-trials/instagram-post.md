## Hook

Most people get married hoping to be happy. The ones still married 50 years later stopped chasing that a while ago.

## Caption body

This is "Enduring Love; Strength through Trials," a piece I made last year.

I started it not long after hearing about a young couple who never got the chance to grow old together. I couldn't stop thinking about the fifty years they wouldn't get to have.

So I painted an elderly Jewish couple instead — wrapped around each other, faces that have earned every line on them. Behind them — Jerusalem. A city torn down and rebuilt more times than anyone can count.

That's the whole painting, really. The couple and the city, mirroring each other. Both broken open by hard seasons. Both still standing — not untouched, but renewed.

I don't think that's an accident of two people staying happy for 50 years straight.

"Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her." (Ephesians 5:25) That's not a happiness formula — it's a picture of a love that stays, on purpose, through the parts nobody photographs.

I love watching couples who've actually done this.

One more thing I can't stop thinking about: in Poland, couples married 50 years receive the Medal za Długoletnie Pożycie Małżeńskie — the Medal for Long Marital Life — given in the name of the President of Poland. A whole country decided lasting love deserves an honor.

I wish we did that here.
