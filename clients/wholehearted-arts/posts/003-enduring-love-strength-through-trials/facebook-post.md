## Hook

I want to tell you about a painting I made last year called "Enduring Love; Strength through Trials" — and about why I can't stop thinking about long marriages lately.

## Body

I started this piece not long after hearing about a young couple who never got the chance to grow old together. I couldn't stop thinking about the fifty years they wouldn't get to have.

So I painted an elderly Jewish couple instead, holding each other. Their faces are weathered — every year they've lived through is right there on their skin. Behind them, I painted Jerusalem's skyline, the temple structure rising up in silhouette. A city that's been knocked down and rebuilt more times than anyone can count.

That's really the whole idea. The couple and the city are doing the same thing. Both broken open by hard seasons. Both still standing — not because nothing ever touched them, but because they were rebuilt, over and over, on purpose.

I don't think fifty years of marriage happens because two people just stayed happy the whole time. I think it happens because they decided to build something together, even through the parts that weren't easy.

There's a verse I kept coming back to while I worked on this piece: "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her" (Ephesians 5:25). That's not really a formula for a good mood — it's a picture of a love that stays, on purpose, so that it can show something to everyone watching.

Here's something that made my whole week when I learned about it: in Poland, couples who reach fifty years of marriage are given the Medal za Długoletnie Pożycie Małżeńskie — the Medal for Long Marital Life — awarded in the name of the President of Poland. An entire country decided that lasting love deserves an actual honor.

I wish we did that here. I really do.

If you know a couple who's been through the hard parts and stayed — married 30, 40, 50 years — I'd love to hear their story in the comments.
