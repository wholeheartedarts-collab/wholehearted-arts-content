## Hook
Somewhere along the way, most of us trade wonder for competence. We stop asking "what is that?" and start assuming we already know.

My newest piece is my argument against that trade.

## Body
It's called "Never Stop Wonder" — 36x36, built in layers: florals, scribbles, bursts of light, a dark ground underneath so every color has somewhere to rise from.

I didn't plan every mark. That was the point. I wanted it to feel like thoughts and prayers and questions all blooming at once — not controlled, but alive.

There's a verse I keep coming back to while I paint pieces like this one:

"Truly, I say to you, unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven." — Matthew 18:3

That's not a call to be naive. Children aren't naive — they're just not finished pretending they already have it figured out. They still ask "why." They still stop to look.

The Psalms describe that same posture toward God's own work: "Great are the works of the LORD, studied by all who delight in them." (Psalm 111:2) Studied. Delighted in. Not glanced at.

That's what I wanted this canvas to ask of whoever stands in front of it — not "do you get it," but "will you stay long enough to notice something."

## CTA
If you've felt that trade happen in your own life — wonder quietly replaced by "I already know" — I'd love to hear where you noticed it. And if this piece is speaking to you, I'm always glad to talk about where it could live.
