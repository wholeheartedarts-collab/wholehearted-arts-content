## Executive summary

"Blessed Family" is a small, high-gloss acrylic-and-mica piece from
Agata's Studio Collection with no artist statement in the catalog beyond
its medium and size. Rather than invent a personal story, this post pairs
the painting's title directly with Psalm 128:1-4 (ESV) — a short psalm
about the blessing of a God-fearing household, read at Jewish and
Christian weddings for centuries — which fits the client's confirmed
content pillars (faith, family, marriage) without requiring any
unconfirmed personal narrative.

## Key points

- The painting's only concrete facts are medium, size, finish, and price
  — there is no scripture, quote, or story written into the piece itself
  (unlike several other Studio pieces, e.g. post 040's Psalm 127 piece).
- Psalm 128 is a wisdom psalm traditionally categorized as one of the
  "Songs of Ascents" (Psalms 120-134), historically associated with
  pilgrimage to Jerusalem.
- Psalm 128:1 (ESV): "Blessed is everyone who fears the LORD, who walks in
  his ways!" — confirmed via WebSearch against ESV text (ESV wording
  appears consistently across esv.org and other ESV-text sources in
  search results; direct fetch of esv.org/biblegateway.com/biblehub.com is
  blocked by sandbox egress, per the run's instructions, so verification
  relied on WebSearch result snippets, not a live fetch).
- Psalm 128:3 (ESV): "Your wife will be like a fruitful vine within your
  house; your children will be like olive shoots around your table." —
  confirmed via WebSearch; multiple ESV-labeled sources (esv.org verse
  page, Bible Gateway ESV 2016 text, Bible Study Tools ESV text) agree on
  this exact wording.
- Psalm 128:4 (ESV): "Behold, thus shall the man be blessed who fears the
  LORD." — confirmed via WebSearch (esv.org fragment).
- Note on translation variance (for the writer's awareness, not for the
  post): other translations render verse 3's "olive shoots" differently
  (NKJV "olive plants," CSB/NLT "young olive trees," NIV close to ESV) —
  staying with the ESV wording specifically, since that is the translation
  actually confirmed.
