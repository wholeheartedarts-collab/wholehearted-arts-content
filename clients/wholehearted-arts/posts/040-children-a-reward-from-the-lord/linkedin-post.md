## Hook
There's a prayer written into this painting — not as a caption underneath it, but stitched right into the canvas itself, in white pen.

## Body
It's a quiet scene: a father and mother, praying together over the child they haven't met yet.

Worked into the fabric and twine around them, in white pen, is Psalm 127:

"Unless the LORD builds the house, those who build it labor in vain... It is in vain that you rise up early and go late to rest, eating the bread of anxious toil... Behold, children are a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb a reward."

I keep coming back to the first half of that psalm as much as the line everyone quotes. Before it ever gets to children, it's about the striving — the early mornings, the late nights, the anxious toil — and how none of it actually builds anything on its own.

Then it just settles. "He gives to his beloved sleep." And only after that: a family isn't something you achieve. It's given.

That's part of why I wanted this piece to have real texture instead of a flat illustration — fabric and twine worked right into the acrylic. Things literally woven together, the way I think a family gets woven together: slowly, imperfectly, by hand.
