# Research: Protect My Heart (Studio Collection painting)

## Executive summary

The painting "Protect My Heart" (oil on canvas, 18"x24", framed; Studio
Collection) depicts a woman standing as guardian of her own heart — the
artist's product description frames it as a reminder that shielding
yourself from the world and staying open to love are not opposites, but
a balance. That tension — guard vs. stay open — maps directly onto
Proverbs 4:23, a well-attested, easily verified scripture passage, and
onto the client's "healing, emotion, marriages, family, overcoming
struggles while staying faithful" content pillars. This gives a solid,
sourced scriptural anchor plus the artist's own stated intent; broader
secular psychology research on vulnerability exists but is not strong
enough in quality to lean on for specific claims (see Limitations below),
so the post should stay anchored in scripture + the artist's own words
rather than cite a specific study.

## Risks, counterarguments, or limitations

- A web search for psychology research on "vulnerability vs.
  self-protection in relationships" returned mostly self-help/blog
  content (Psych Central, Psychology Today, a personality-test company
  survey) rather than a single clear, citable peer-reviewed study. One
  Psych Central piece references a "2019 study" linking supportive
  partner responses to vulnerability with greater intimacy, but without
  enough detail here to verify the primary source directly — too weak to
  state as a sourced fact in a client post. **Recommendation: do not
  cite specific psychology research/statistics in either draft.** Keep
  the post anchored in scripture + the artist's own description of the
  work, which is both stronger and avoids any fabrication risk.
  [SOURCED as "search found weak/unverifiable evidence" — the weakness
  itself is the finding here]
- "Guard your heart" language is sometimes used in purity-culture
  contexts in ways that can feel restrictive or gendered; the artist's
  own description already avoids that framing (explicitly pairs
  "shield" with "remain open to... connection"), so the post should
  preserve that balance rather than flattening it into a one-sided
  "protect yourself" message. [INFERENCE — a framing risk to avoid, not
  a sourced claim]
- No personal story, incident, or quote from Agata beyond the catalog
  description is available for this painting — per the no-fabrication
  rule, the post must not invent a backstory, and should stay somewhat
  image-led/reflective rather than anecdotal.

## Possible content angles

1. **Scripture-anchored read of the painting** — Proverbs 4:23 as the
   lens for what the woman in the painting is doing; guarding what
   shapes you, not closing yourself off.
2. **The paradox itself** — shielding and staying open aren't opposites;
   a short, reflective post built around that tension, addressed to
   anyone (collector or business-automation audience) currently guarding
   against burnout/disappointment while trying to stay open to good
   things.
3. **Process/craft angle** — oil on canvas, framed, the physical choices
   behind a single-figure painting — thinner angle given how little
   process detail exists in the source description; usable as texture,
   not the main angle.
4. **Direct, short devotional-style read** — verse + one or two lines of
   reflection + the painting, minimal commentary. Good fit for Instagram.
5. **Marriage/family framing** — guarding your heart *within* a close
   relationship rather than from the world generally — plausible given
   the client's "marriages, family" pillar, but the source description
   doesn't specify a relational context, so this angle would need to stay
   general/invitational ("maybe this is a marriage, a friendship, your
   own healing") rather than asserting a specific relationship, to avoid
   putting words in Agata's mouth about a work she hasn't personally
   commented on.
