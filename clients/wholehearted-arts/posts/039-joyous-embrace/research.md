## The story, as Agata's own product description tells it

Quoting the catalog description in full (this is Agata's own writing,
not an invented backstory):

> "What a blessing! This painting got damaged during my studio move but
> it only helped me to redesign it! It never looked quite finished to me
> before! I guess the mature idea of it was still growing in my heart and
> it came to be now! It has brighter colors, bolder patterns and it is
> vibrantly alive and full of joy!"

Key facts drawn directly from this:
- The piece existed in an earlier form that Agata was not satisfied with
  ("never looked quite finished").
- It was physically damaged during a studio relocation.
- Rather than discarding it, she reworked it — resulting in brighter
  colors, bolder patterns, and (her words) a piece that is "vibrantly
  alive and full of joy."
- She frames the outcome as a blessing, not just a repair.

No further personal history, exhibition history, or anecdote is given in
the catalog entry, and none should be invented — this brief sticks to
what the entry itself states.

## Content angle

**Core theme: redemption through disruption — something broken becoming
more alive, not despite the damage but partly through reworking it.**
This sits squarely in the profile's confirmed art pillars: faith,
emotion, healing, and overcoming struggles while staying faithful — all
without needing an invented scripture citation, since Agata's own
description doesn't cite one for this piece.

### Possible angles
1. **Redemption/restoration angle** — the literal story (damaged →
   reworked → more alive than before) as a metaphor for how disruption in
   a season of life doesn't have to mean loss; it can be the thing that
   makes room for something truer. Strongest, most direct angle; stays
   fully inside what Agata actually said.
2. **"Unfinished" angle** — she says the piece "never looked quite
   finished" before the damage. A reflection on sitting with
   not-yet-finished work (in art or in faith) until it's ready, rather
   than forcing it.
3. **Process/texture angle** — mixed media, acrylic + oil pastel on thick
   cardboard, bold color and pattern — a more sensory, studio-practice
   post for art-collector audience, lighter on narrative, more on
   material and look.
4. **Small-and-available angle** — only 1 in stock, modest price point
   ($350) relative to much of the Atelier catalog — could be framed as an
   approachable entry point for a new collector, but this is a soft CTA
   at most per the profile's "no hard sales pitch" default.

**Angle selected for both platforms:** #1 (redemption/restoration),
optionally paired with a widely known, independently verified scripture
about God turning mourning/brokenness into joy — presented explicitly as
a thematic echo the writer is drawing, not as a claim that Agata herself
cited this verse for this piece (she didn't, per the catalog text).
