## Hook
A painting got damaged during my studio move. I almost lost it completely.

Reworking it is the best thing that ever happened to it.

## Body
Here's what actually happened, start to finish.

I had a piece I'd been sitting with for a while. It never looked quite finished to me — something about it felt unresolved, even though I couldn't say exactly what.

Then, during a studio move, it got damaged.

My first instinct was to see it as a loss. Instead, the damage forced a decision I'd been avoiding: redo it.

What came back was brighter. Bolder patterns, brighter colors, and — this is the part that surprised me — it finally felt alive in a way the original never did. Not fixed. Transformed.

I keep thinking about how often this is also just... how faith works. Something breaks. You don't choose the timing. And instead of being the end of the piece, it turns out to be the thing that finally lets it become what it was always meant to be.

There's a line in Psalm 30 about mourning being turned into dancing — grief swapped out for joy, not patched over but genuinely replaced. That's closer to what happened on this canvas than "repair" is.

I named the finished piece "Joyous Embrace." Mixed media — acrylic and oil pastel on thick cardboard, 24" x 19". There's only one, and it's part of my Studio Collection.
