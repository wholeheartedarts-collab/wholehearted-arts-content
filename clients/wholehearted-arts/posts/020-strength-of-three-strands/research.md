# Research: The Strength of Three Strands

## Executive summary

"The Strength of Three Strands" is an Atelier Collection mixed-media
painting by Agata May'kowska built around Ecclesiastes 4:12 ("a cord of
three strands is not quickly broken"), applied to marriage as a covenant
between husband, wife, and God. The artist's own product description
frames the piece as a study on covenant marriage — not just a physical
union but a union of "the whole man and whole woman (their bodies, their
hearts and their minds) and God" — and describes her use of circles
(feminine) and squares (masculine) as recurring symbols of complementary
difference that "fit" together only through mutual growth. The verse
itself, and its common Christian application to marriage (God as the
"third strand"), are both well-attested and appropriate for this
client's faith/marriage/family content pillar. No politics involved.

## Key points

- Painting title: "The Strength of Three Strands." Atelier Collection.
  Mixed media on canvas, 16"x40", depth 1.5", unframed, dark brown canvas
  sides. (Source: painting-catalog.json product listing, drawn live from
  Squarespace Commerce.)
- Artist's stated theme (direct from her own product description): a
  "study on covenant marriage" — the covenant is "not just a covenant of
  flesh but the whole man and whole woman (their bodies, their hearts and
  their minds) and God." She writes that Scripture "helps us protect
  marriage and grow together to fulfill its purpose."
- Artist's stated visual symbolism (her own words): circles represent the
  feminine, squares represent the masculine; she paints both shapes
  mixed/interlocking to show how each must grow to "fit" the other —
  "I must become a greater circle to fit in the square and then the
  square must become bigger to fit me in. This requires both of us to
  grow in love for Christ and for one another."
- Biblical basis: Ecclesiastes 4:9-12, and specifically verse 12, "a cord
  of three strands is not quickly broken" (independently confirmed across
  multiple translations — see sources.md). The passage's plain meaning is
  about the general strength of partnership/companionship (two are better
  than one, and three stronger still) — it is not a marriage-specific
  passage in its original context.
- Common Christian application to marriage: the "third strand" is widely
  and specifically taught as representing God in a marriage, with the
  other two strands representing husband and wife — used in wedding
  ceremonies (sometimes literally, with a braided-cord ritual) and
  described across multiple independent faith-teaching sources (see
  sources.md). This is a genuine, widely-attested interpretive tradition,
  not something invented for this post — but it should be presented as
  an interpretation/application of the verse (which the artist herself
  makes), not as if the verse's original text is only, or literally,
  about marriage.
- Availability: catalog's `availability_note` flags the listing as
  showing zero stock — "likely already sold." No price should be quoted
  and the post must not imply it is currently purchasable.
