## Executive summary

"Prayerful Rose or Jeremiah" is a small (10"x8", framed) mixed-media
piece — fabric and acrylic — built directly around Jeremiah 31:33-34,
God's promise of a new covenant written on the heart rather than kept on
stone. The content angle is simple and image-led: a small, intimate
piece carrying a large promise — that forgiveness and transformation
aren't imposed from outside, but placed within.

## Key points

- The painting's own catalog description quotes Jeremiah 31:33-34 (ESV)
  in full: God's declaration that he will make a new covenant with the
  house of Israel, put his law within them, write it on their hearts,
  and "remember their sin no more."
- Catalog description for this SKU (SQ8832402): "Mixed Media - Fabric and
  Acrylic," "Size 10"/8" plus frame," "Framed." **Correction for the
  record:** an earlier draft of the posts mistakenly borrowed an "old
  restored frame with some wear and tear" detail from a different,
  similarly-worded catalog entry ("Seeking His Will," a different SKU) —
  caught during fact-check before either post was finalized and removed.
  This piece's catalog description is simply "Framed," nothing more
  specific about the frame's condition.
- This passage is part of the "new covenant" prophecy in Jeremiah 31 —
  one of the most quoted Old Testament passages in the New Testament.
  Hebrews 8:8-12 quotes it directly (verified via WebSearch: ESV.org and
  multiple secondary sources confirm Hebrews 8 reproduces Jeremiah 31:31-34
  almost word-for-word, applying it to Christ's new covenant). I'm not
  using the "longest OT quotation in the NT" claim some secondary sources
  make, since I could not verify that specific superlative against a
  primary source — leaving it out rather than risk an unsupported stat.
- The piece itself is modest in scale and materials (mixed media, fabric
  and acrylic, 10"x8", framed) — this supports an image-led,
  unpretentious angle rather than a grand statement.

## Possible content angles

1. **The heart vs. the stone** — contrast law written on tablets of
   stone (the old covenant) with law written on the heart (the new) —
   the painting as a small, physical meditation on an internal change.
2. **Small piece, big promise** — lean into the painting's modest scale
   (10x8") against the size of what it depicts (a covenant that remakes
   a relationship with God).
3. **"I will remember their sin no more"** — focus the whole post on the
   forgiveness half of the promise rather than the covenant mechanics —
   more emotionally direct, less theological explainer.
4. Image-led, short caption focused on process (fabric + acrylic, framed)
   with the scripture as a caption rather than the subject of an
   argument — lowest risk of sounding like a sermon.

Angle used for drafts: a blend of #1 and #3 — short, personal framing of
the contrast (stone vs. heart) landing on the forgiveness line, kept
brief per the risk above.
