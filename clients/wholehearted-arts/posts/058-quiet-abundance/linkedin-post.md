## Hook
There's a painting on my easel right now that's only 10 inches square. No bold centerpiece, no loud color — and it's the one I keep going back to.

## Body
It's called "Quiet Abundance." Hydrangeas, loosely gathered in a terracotta vessel, in warm gold, soft lavender, violet, blush, and sky blue. Mixed media on a wooden canvas board, textured enough that the light shifts it a little through the day.

Hydrangeas carry an old, inconsistent symbolism, and I like that it's inconsistent. In Japanese flower tradition they're often tied to heartfelt gratitude. Other traditions read them as abundance, or prosperity — and, depending on who you ask, even boastfulness, since one bloom can carry so many petals and so few seeds. No single fixed meaning. Just a flower that different people have read differently for a long time.

I've also been sitting with something less poetic: there's real, if modest, research suggesting that short stretches of quiet — low noise, no demands — are linked to a calmer body and clearer mind. Nothing dramatic. Just a nudge that slowing down isn't nothing.

This piece is my attempt at something small enough to actually sit with. Not a showpiece. A place to rest your eyes for a minute, and maybe feel grateful for whatever's quietly abundant in your own life right now.
