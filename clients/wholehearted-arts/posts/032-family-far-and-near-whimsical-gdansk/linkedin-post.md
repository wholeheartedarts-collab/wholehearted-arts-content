## Post

I have family on two sides of an ocean, and I've stopped trying to rank them.

This is "Whimsical Gdańsk." Six inches square, a city glowing after dusk, crooked rooftops and golden windows built from fabric, paint, and metallic accents. It's the Poland I carry around. Not a map. A feeling.

Loving people from far away is a skill nobody teaches you. It's phone calls at strange hours and knowing the weather in a city you don't live in. It's missing things, birthdays and hospital visits and ordinary Tuesdays, and showing up anyway in every way that's left.

And then there's the family that grew around me here, in America. The people who became home when home was an ocean away. For a long time I thought loving them fully meant loving the other side less. It doesn't. You don't split love. You learn it twice.

"If I rise on the wings of the dawn, if I settle on the far side of the sea, even there your hand will guide me, your hand will hold me fast." (Psalm 139:9-10)

Far side of the sea. I read that verse differently than I used to.

If you're loving someone across a distance right now, a country, a time zone, a hospital wing, I'd genuinely love to hear how you do it.
