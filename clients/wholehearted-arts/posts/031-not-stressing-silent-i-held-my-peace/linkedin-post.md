## Post

Not everything urgent is yours to carry.

That's a hard sentence for anyone who runs their own work. Every email, every "quick question," every thing that could go wrong arrives wearing the same uniform, as if each one is the emergency.

Most of them aren't. And the few that are still don't get better when I carry them in my chest for three days.

Philippians 4 doesn't say "don't have problems." It says: "Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus." (Philippians 4:6-7)

Present the request. Then let something stronger than willpower guard the mind. I notice the verse doesn't promise the problem leaves. It promises the peace arrives first.

Behind these words is a blurred detail of my painting "Silent, I Held My Peace." A woman with her eyes closed, holding still inside a lot of noise. She isn't calm because the noise stopped. She's calm inside it.

What's the thing you're carrying this week that was never yours to carry? Sometimes naming it is half of setting it down.
