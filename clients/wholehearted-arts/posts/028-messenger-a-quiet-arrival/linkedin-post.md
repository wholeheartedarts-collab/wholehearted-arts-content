## Hook

Most of the biggest moments in scripture don't arrive with thunder.

A dove. A leaf. A whisper after the earthquake was already over.

## Body

I just finished a piece called "Messenger" — a solitary bird sitting
inside a layered, textured landscape. No scripture written into this
one, no big dramatic gesture. Just a quiet arrival.

While I was painting it, I kept thinking about how often God shows up
that way in the Bible. Not in the noise — in what comes after it.

After the wind, the earthquake, and the fire, Elijah doesn't meet God in
any of them. He meets him in what the ESV translates as "the sound of a
low whisper" (1 Kings 19:12) — the "still small voice," if you grew up on
the KJV.

Noah's dove doesn't come back with a shout. It comes back with one
olive leaf in its beak, and that's how he knows the water is receding
(Genesis 8:11).

And at Jesus's baptism, the Spirit doesn't arrive like fire from the
sky. It comes down "like a dove" (Matthew 3:16).

Three completely different moments. Same pattern: the message that
actually matters tends to arrive small, and it's easy to miss if you're
only watching for something loud.

That's the feeling I wanted to leave room for in this piece — not a
scripture spelled out on the canvas, but a way of looking that asks you
to slow down and notice.
