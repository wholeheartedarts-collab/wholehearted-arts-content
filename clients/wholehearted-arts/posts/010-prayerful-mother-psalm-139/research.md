## Executive summary

"Prayerful Mother - Psalm 139" is a large (36"x24" plus frame) acrylic
painting by Agata May'kowska in the Studio Collection, priced at
$1,500.00 (the catalog flags this as "listed as special sale" — the sale
status/price should be confirmed with Agata before it's stated as a firm
current price in any post). Per the catalog, it incorporates words from
Psalm 139 and is described by the artist as "a prayer for a mom and a
child," exploring the wonder and worry that come with pregnancy. Psalm
139 is one of the most widely cited psalms on the theme of being fully
known and formed by God before birth — a well-documented, doctrinally
mainstream scriptural anchor that fits the client's confirmed content
pillars (faith, family, prayer, praising God) without touching politics.

## Key points

- **The painting (per catalog, unverified live but a real product
  listing):** acrylic painting incorporating words of Psalm 139, 36"x24"
  plus frame, Studio Collection, priced at $1,500 ("listed as special
  sale" per catalog — flagged for Agata to confirm before any post states
  it as a current firm price). Grey wood frame.
- **Artist's own framing (per catalog, treat as a direct quote from the
  site, not to be altered or "corrected"):** "A prayer for a mom and a
  child." The catalog also quotes: "we all were uniquely made by God on
  our mothers womb" and "he has a good plan. All is written in his
  book." These read as Agata's own phrasing (consistent with her Polish
  heritage/English-as-a-learned-language voice elsewhere in the catalog)
  — used as a direct quote, not smoothed into "corrected" English, since
  altering a person's own quoted words would misrepresent them.
- **Psalm 139 — verified text (ESV, via ESV.org/BibleHub, WebSearch
  2026-08-21):**
  - v.13-14: "For you formed my inward parts; you knitted me together in
    my mother's womb. I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully
    made. Wonderful are your works; my soul knows it very well."
  - v.16: "Your eyes saw my unformed substance; in your book were
    written, every one of them, the days that were formed for me, when
    as yet there was none of them."
- The artist's quoted line "All is written in his book" lines up
  specifically with v.16's "in your book were written... the days that
  were formed for me" — not just a general theme, but a direct echo of
  that specific verse. This is a real, checkable connection, not an
  invented one.
- Psalm 139 as a whole is widely used, across denominations, as a
  pregnancy/parenting-prayer text and as a foundational passage for the
  doctrine that human life and identity are formed and known by God
  before birth — this is well-documented, not a fringe reading.
