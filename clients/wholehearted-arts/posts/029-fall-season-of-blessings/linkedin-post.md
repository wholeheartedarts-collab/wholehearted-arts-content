## Post

Every October I'm reminded that the most productive thing a tree does all year is let go.

I painted "Season of Blessings" as a grove of birches pressed close together: white trunks, a canopy turning from green to gold, a path curving off toward open fields. Fabric and acrylic, built up in layers and splatter until the surface has real texture.

Up close it looks like a mess. Step back and it's the most beautiful thing the year does.

I think about that a lot in a season when most of us are sprinting toward year-end. Not everything that's finishing is failing. Some of it is just finished. A project, a role, a version of yourself that got you here but doesn't need to come along.

"There is a time for everything, and a season for every activity under the heavens." (Ecclesiastes 3:1)

The birches don't negotiate with the calendar. They change on time, and they're glorious while they do it.

What are you letting go of this fall? Or what's finishing that you can finally call finished? I'd genuinely love to hear.
